Algebraic–entropic research programme

AEFH v7.3: mathematical core and physical constructions

AEFH v7.3 is an independent research programme built around an entropy-based operator functional. This page is an English overview of the current revision; the full formal manuscript is available in Russian.

Status and scope

The manuscript separates mathematical statements about the functional from physical hypotheses. Its numerical tests check selected finite-dimensional matrix examples and regression properties of the implementation. They do not prove results for arbitrary von Neumann factors, settle domain questions for unbounded operators, or establish that a proposed physical model describes the real Universe.

The v7.3 page identifies remaining proof work, including a full justification of a limiting step in A.5 and a more detailed finite-dimensional necessity witness for A.4. These are not presented as solved merely because the numerical test bench runs.

1. The operator functional

For normal states ρ and σ, the research programme studies the family

Iα(ρ, σ) = SAraki(ρ ‖ σ) + α · (1 − F1/2(ρ, σ))

where the first term is Araki relative entropy and the second uses the Petz–Hellinger affinity at parameter 1/2. The precise conventions and operator-algebraic setting are specified in the full manuscript.

2. Mathematical core

A.1 · Local stability

Local threshold 4

The manuscript studies the local quadratic stability threshold and distinguishes commuting from non-commuting perturbation directions.

A.2 · Global inequality

Global threshold 2

A separate global threshold is stated for the entropy–affinity combination, with assumptions that must be read together with the theorem.

A.3 · Finite dimension

Exact γN threshold

The finite-dimensional analysis gives a dimension-dependent threshold and an asymptotic expansion.

A.4 · Monotonicity

Data-processing inequality

The manuscript examines monotonicity under quantum channels and explicitly notes the proof detail that still needs a fuller witness construction.

A.5 · Resolvents

Resolvent identities in a type II∞ model

Integral and resolvent identities are developed in a semifinite setting. The manuscript distinguishes analytic claims from finite-matrix numerical checks.

Numerical audit

21-test test bench

The published v7.3 page reports 21 unit-test cases. Passing numerical tests is evidence about the tested implementation and samples, not a general mathematical proof.

3. Physical constructions

The revision presents ten physical constructions, including model-dependent directions in gravity, cosmology, and particle physics. They should be read as conditional constructions: each relies on additional assumptions and parameters, and none follows automatically from the operator inequalities alone.

4. What would strengthen the programme?

  • Complete the remaining analytic proof obligations, with explicit domains, convergence modes, and hypotheses.
  • Keep numerical tests independent where possible: compare different algorithms against known exact cases and actively search for counterexamples.
  • State physical predictions before fitting them, then compare them with independent observational data and competing models.
  • Make the distinction between a proved theorem, a numerical consistency check, and a speculative physical interpretation visible on every research page.

This page is a concise English research overview, not a substitute for the complete statement of theorems and proofs. The Russian v7.3 manuscript is the authoritative full-text version currently linked by the project.
